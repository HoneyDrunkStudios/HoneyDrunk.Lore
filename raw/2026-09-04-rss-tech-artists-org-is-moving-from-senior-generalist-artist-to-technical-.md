I have 12 years of experience in computer graphics, including five years at a large mobile game studio, where I currently work as a Senior Generalist Artist.
At the studio, I handle almost anything a project might need: 3D modeling, rigging, animation, illustration, 2D rigging and animation, level design, asset implementation, system setup, Maya and Unity tools, shaders, documentation, and more.
I enjoy the variety and working on different kinds of tasks. Lately, though, I have been feeling less motivated because my current company does not have many projects that offer major technical challenges or aim for high-end visual quality.
I have been considering pursuing a Technical Artist position at an AAA studio or at least at a studio developing games for consoles so I can work on projects that better match my interests.
However, after spending so many years doing a little bit of everything and, above all, solving problems, I am finding it difficult to choose a specialization. At the moment, I am leaning toward improving my Python skills and perhaps learning C++ for Unreal Engine. Most of my experience is with Unity and C#, but I do not see many positions at larger studios asking for that particular combination.
With the increased use of AI, I have also been able to tackle more technical problems than before and create more shaders, tools, and systems. I am unsure how studios view this. Is using AI as part of a Technical Artist’s workflow generally frowned upon?
I would love to hear your thoughts on the current state of Technical Art at larger studios. Do you think moving from a Generalist Artist role into Technical Art would be a worthwhile transition? I am considering this direction partly because I rarely see generalist positions at larger studios.
I want to move toward projects that are closer to the kind of work I genuinely enjoy, but I am also concerned about the risks of changing career paths, especially a possible salary decrease. I also worry that, by specializing in Technical Art, my 10+ years of experience as a generalist might be undervalued.

even though you might move away from generalist and doing a bit of everything you’d be surprised at how much the general knowledge helps you as a tech-artist and how much general tasks you actually get to do (allthough this does depend on the studio you work at)
the more knowledge you have of other peoples tasks and workflows the better you would get at building tools specialised at UX design for them.
also depending on the studio there are different takes on how and where AI can take place. in some its extremely frowned upon and others adopt it completely as in vibe code driven tools etc. with the middle ground being a overpowered google that excels at finding answers to specific problems and with capabilities of converting documentation into tutorials. its really difficult to know the stance of a studio untill you ask them directly or check what their public remarks about it are.
