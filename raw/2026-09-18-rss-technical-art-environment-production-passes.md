# Assembling Abandoned Middle Eastern Courtyard in 3D Using Pass-Based Workflow

Capture format: attributed summary of fetched public written content; not a full-text reproduction.

Irmak Çavun describes building a modular courtyard through representative scene passes before final polish. Early models, textures, lighting, and composition are evaluated together in-engine, allowing direction changes before detailed asset work becomes expensive.

The workflow combines larger modular pieces with smaller snapping elements. Substance 3D Designer materials expose aging and damage controls; a reusable tile-damage generator supports rapid pattern changes.

A layered Unreal master material combines RGB masks, vertex paint, and texture paint. Variants reuse underlying materials rather than requiring unique textures for each object. Most scene assets share that system, with water, sky, and decals as exceptions.

Polish uses a small decal set, blending, and lighting refinement. Individual light sources are isolated for adjustment before being evaluated together again.

Lore relevance: a reusable art-production process for reducing iteration waste and expanding asset variation. Reported efficiency is a single artist's experience, and this portfolio breakdown does not establish runtime performance or a general rule against unique textures.
