# Desirable Patina: How to Make Realistic Rust in 3D

Attributed summary of the fetched article.

Material artist Loic Anquetil describes building procedural rust in Substance 3D Designer by studying corrosion's physical structure before emphasizing color. Close-up references and personally collected photographs guided granularity, relief, and small variations.

A foundational pitted-rust material established the relationship between breakup patterns, height, roughness, and color. Later variants added peeling paint, dirt, and stages of corrosion. Exploratory grayscale noise combinations were rebuilt into controlled procedural graphs once useful patterns emerged.

The reusable result matters as much as its appearance: expose parameters, group nodes logically, and keep graphs readable for other artists. Modifying existing noise graphs is presented as practical production work rather than a requirement to rebuild every component.

HoneyDrunk application: develop a small, inspectable material library with controlled variation and reusable structural foundations. Source confidence: practitioner interview tied to a commercial asset collection; no independent performance or cross-engine compatibility tests are supplied.
