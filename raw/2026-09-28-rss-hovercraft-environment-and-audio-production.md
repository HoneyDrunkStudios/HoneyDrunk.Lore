# Source capture

Volodymyr Donchenko describes moving an existing 3ds Max asset collection into an Unreal Engine environment. Reusing concept assets and an established lighting palette reduced repeated design work. Blender tooling and baked vertex colors supported procedural material workflows during import.

A flight template and a separate AI template required manual adaptation. The production account also separates spatial sound from continuous background music: location-based audio uses attenuation, while a looping score follows its own playback setup. The soundtrack was composed, mixed, exported, and tested inside the scene.

HoneyDrunk application: prototype the asset-to-engine path and the visual/audio direction together before expanding a large environment. Track template adaptation and workstation limits as production costs.

This is a creator's technical-demo account, not a shipped-game benchmark. Its broad claims about rendering performance lack controlled measurements and should not establish an engine performance budget.
